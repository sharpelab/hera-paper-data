**Read: one Fermi pocket.** Four of the five densities return **exactly one** unflagged SdH
tone; the fifth (n = +1.99) has all its tones flagged marginal or harmonic and yields none.
**No density yields two.** The two highest-contrast traces — n = +0.99 and +1.49 × 10¹² cm⁻²,
contrast 19.4 and 8.3 — give f/f_full = **0.835** and **0.834**, and a fit through the
origin over |n| > 0.9 gives 8.62 T per 10¹² cm⁻² against the 10.34 full-Onsager slope.

**That is the discriminating fact, and it needs no calibration.** Two Fermi pockets would
put the dominant tone near 0.7× the full Onsager value *and* add a second tone near 0.3×,
both scaling linearly with n. Neither appears. A Bernal (ABAB) tetralayer at D = 0 has two
bilayer-like bands with different masses, both crossing E_F at any finite density, and would
show exactly that two-tone structure. A rhombohedral (ABCA) stack has a single quartic band
and shows what we see.

**An independent consistency check falls out.** Reading the 0.834 ratio as a single 4-fold
degenerate pocket gives α = 1.199 for the absolute density scale. The Landau fan's minima
ladder — a completely different observable, from run 4 — gave α = 1.166. **The two agree to
2.8 %.** That does not break the α/g degeneracy (both routes still measure only the product),
but it does say the two datasets are describing the same Fermi surface consistently.

**Caveats.** The two low-|n| traces are unreliable and I am not using them in the fit:
n = +0.49 and −0.81 carry too few oscillations over the field range, and their apparent
ratios (1.02, 1.36) scatter accordingly. The hole side reaches only −0.81 × 10¹² cm⁻²
because the top-gate limit bounds it, so the pocket count is really established on the
electron side. And a *second* pocket much smaller than the first — carrying, say, under 10 %
of the carriers — would produce a tone below 1.5 T that the degree-5 background removal
would eat; this measurement excludes two comparable pockets, not a small satellite.
