## sweeping silicon back gate systematically (-40 to 40 V)

```python
s = sweep.AsyncStation(mc_2)

s.ramp(sr830_1.amplitude, 1)
s.ramp(k_tg.volt, 0.5)
s.ramp(k_bg.volt, -8)
s.ramp(k_si.volt, -40)
s.ramp(mag.field, 0.00)
time.sleep(10)

s.add_comment('MC2, Vtg=0.5V, sweeping Vsi (slow) and Vbg (fast)')
s.add_comment(grab_metadata())

s.fp(temperature)
s.fp(k_bg.curr)
s.fp(k_tg.curr)
for sr, gain in sr830s:
    s.fp(sr.X, gain)
    s.fp(sr.P)

s.plot(k_bg.volt, k_bg.curr)
s.plot('time', k_tg.curr)
s.plot('time', k_si.curr)

s.megasweep(k_si.volt, np.linspace(-40, 40, 41), k_bg.volt, np.linspace(-8, 6.5, 201), slow_delay=5, fast_delay=1)

k_bg.volt(0)
k_tg.volt(0)
```

The section spans both flavors of Si BG survey:
- **Flavor A** (cells 45–54): four megasweeps at fixed V_si values (−20, −15, +20 V) — small (n, D) maps to compare device behavior at each operating point.
- **Flavor B** (cells 56, 58): systematic 2D sweep with `k_si.volt` as slow axis (41 points over −40 to +40 V), `k_bg.volt` fast (201 points). At fixed V_tg = 0.5 V, fixed V_bg starting point. `slow_delay=5` is conservative because Si BG ramps need more settle time than the front gates. Cell 58 narrows to the negative half (V_si = −40 to −20 V) at higher slow_delay.

Mid-section markdown notes record Aaron's reasoning live ("I calibrated at 0 V on the top gate and at -5 V on the back gate. There it was near the expected current. So let's just let it run") — this is exactly the kind of operator judgment a recipe should preserve.
