# SdH spot check

## What
A fixed-(n, D) magnetic-field sweep over a **low-field window**, designed to resolve
Shubnikov–de Haas / quantum-oscillation **frequency content** at one density — the
onset field and the tone frequencies — by FFT in 1/B. Mechanically this is a
`field-sweep-1d` (see it for the Station / sweep / fp / plot call surface); what
makes it a *spot check* is the design discipline below — the field range, 1/B spacing
Δ(1/B), and settling are chosen to resolve the tones cleanly, and the read is the 1/B
FFT.
It is the cheap 1D precursor to a `landau-fan`: one density, but **full per-point
quality**.

## When
- **Before committing a `landau-fan`.** A spot check pins the **onset** (sets the
  fan's B-range), the **observed tone frequencies** (confirm/correct the fan's
  Δ(1/B)), and the **settling/sensitivity** the fan inherits. A fan is the dense
  version; a couple of clean spot checks de-risk it — and can answer the question
  outright (`investigations/trilayer-stacking-fs-count.md` Step 3).
- Whenever a `field-sweep-1d` or `gatemap` showed QO-like wiggles and you want the
  frequency content at a point, not the whole (n, B) plane.

## Why
A spot check is cheap because it is **one density (1D), not because it is
low-quality**. Its whole value is being acquired *well* — at the settling floor and
Nyquist on the fastest tone — so a clean spot check can be decisive on its own and a
noisier fan cannot overturn it (`landau-fan`). Two knobs carry the quality:

- **Sampling.** The fastest tone sets the finest 1/B period. The reference frequency
  **F_L = (h/g·e)·n** (g = 4) — what a single four-fold pocket carrying all the
  density would give — is an upper bound on the fastest tone; Nyquist it. Undersampling
  the fast tone folds its aliases into the low-frequency band, where they masquerade
  as a small/slow tone (Verify).
- **Settling.** A swept-point dwell has a floor near the lock-in time constant
  (dwell ≥ k·τ; k ≈ 5 at 6 dB/oct, 7–10 at 24 dB/oct). Under-settling **attenuates
  the oscillation amplitude itself** (the filter cannot track the QO) — benign on a
  smooth slope, but it *buries a small tone* (Verify). The floor is non-negotiable
  when the target is a weak tone.

## Recipe
1. **Density + range.** Pick the density — high |n| gives the most periods and the
   clearest tones. Sweep a field window **[B_lo, B_hi]**; the sampling grid follows
   the magnet class:
   - **Field moves cheap** (non-blocking magnet): sample **uniformly in 1/B**
     (`field_invB`) — constant points-per-period, FFT the raw points (no resample).
     `B_lo` is a small positive floor (~0.1–0.3 T) below the expected **onset** (the
     lowest B where oscillations emerge once the MR background is subtracted) —
     uniform-1/B is densest in B at low field, so it captures the onset for free.
   - **Blocking rate-limited magnet** (traverse dominates wall-clock): sample
     **uniformly in B** with **ΔB = Δ(1/B)·B_lo²** (Nyquist held at the low-B edge;
     `sdh_fft` resamples internally). Same wall-clock — the points ride the traverse —
     with ≳30× the per-period averaging at high B and no blind ramps between sparse
     high-B points. ΔB scales as B_lo², so put `B_lo` just below the expected onset,
     not at a deep floor.
2. **Δ(1/B) from F_L.** Compute F_L = (h/4e)·n and Nyquist-resolve it: Δ(1/B) ≤ 1/(2·F_L)
   (prefer 1/3). Set N from the window: N = (1/B_lo − 1/B_hi)/Δ(1/B) + 1 uniform-1/B,
   N = (B_hi − B_lo)/ΔB + 1 uniform-B. This Δ(1/B) (and N) is what a committed fan
   inherits.
3. **δF from the record.** The tone resolution is set by the usable 1/B record above
   onset: **δF = 1/(1/B_onset − 1/B_max)** — tones closer than ~δF merge; trust a
   split only past ~2·δF. On high-T_e rigs the onset, not the field cap, sets δF:
   once 1/B_max ≪ 1/B_onset, raising B_max buys little — the lever is the
   density/side with the lower onset. Compute δF against the tones the claim must
   separate before sweeping.
4. **Lock-in.** Set sensitivity so full scale ≈ I_bias × R_target — a signal at a
   few % of range is coarse-ADC digitized (the grainy-FFT failure). Set the dwell at
   the settling floor (Why); read τ and the filter slope, do not guess them.
5. **The sweep.** Run it as a `field-sweep-1d` (its Recipe is the call surface):
   build the Station, ramp the gates to (n, D), `s.sweep(mag.field, B_vec,
   delay=dwell)` with the step-1 grid, fp the temperature(s) and every lock-in X/P,
   plot the live MR and gate-current leakage.
6. **FFT read.** Re-form Rxx from the TSV (rendered PNGs alias).
   - **Onset** — `hera.analysis.sdh_onset(R, B)` returns an `SdhOnset`: `onset_b` is
     the low-B edge of usable oscillation content (minimum over the tracked tones —
     a light tone that outlives the dominant one carries it), `clip_high_b` the high
     edge. `tones` holds each tone's own track: `freq`, per-tone `onset_b`,
     `snr`/`amp` curves over `b_centers`, `delta_f(b_max)` (that tone's usable-record
     resolution), and flags — `censored` (still above threshold at the record's low
     edge: `onset_b` is the record edge, not a measured die-off), `revived`,
     `marginal`, `near_residual`, and the bidirectional harmonic pair
     `harmonic_of` (this tone sits at k× a lower tone) / `harmonics` (freqs of
     tones sitting at k× of this one — a comb member is flagged from both ends
     regardless of which is stronger). Print `on.summary()` in the read
     cell. Pass `r_full_scale = sensitivity / I_bias` so a soft-clipped channel
     truncates the analysis window (with a warning) instead of feeding a flat-topped
     record to the FFT. Failure raises `SdhOnsetError` naming what was untrackable —
     that is a read result (no usable oscillations), not a prompt to eyeball.
   - **FFT** — `hera.analysis.sdh_fft(R_grid, B, *, b_range=(cutoff, B_max), bg_degree=5)`
     takes `R_grid` as `(n_B, n_fast)` — pass a single trace as `R[:, None]` — removes
     the MR background with a degree-`bg_degree` polynomial **in B** (the background is
     smooth in B, not 1/B), then FFTs in 1/B per column, and returns
     `(freqs, spec[n_fast, n_freq])` with `freqs` in Tesla. It needs a field range wide enough that
     the polynomial captures the background without absorbing the oscillations.
     `cutoff ≥ onset_b`, and often **above** it (Verify). Read the **tone frequencies**
     against F_L.

## Verify
- **Settling — under-settling buries a small tone.** A dwell below the floor
  attenuates the oscillation amplitude (the filter can't track it). On a smooth slope
  that's a benign uniform lag; when the target is a small/weak tone it *erases* the
  feature. Non-negotiable for a weak-tone hunt; bend it only for a slope read on a
  strong signal.
- **Sampling — an undersampled fast tone aliases down.** If Δ(1/B) under-samples the
  fastest tone (≈ F_L), its aliases fold into the low-frequency band and masquerade
  as a small/slow tone. Nyquist the *fast* tone even when the target is the slow one
  (worked case: Examples).
- **Grid-change reproduction separates tones from aliases.** A real tone survives a
  change of sampling scheme (uniform-B vs uniform-1/B); an alias moves with the grid.
- **FFT stability sweep.** Vary `bg_degree` and the window; a real peak **sharpens**
  as the 1/B record lengthens, an alias/artifact **wanders**.
- **Onset is read from the background-subtracted data, not the FFT.** It is the
  low-field point where oscillations emerge in ΔRxx(B) (the MR background removed), not
  a quantity the FFT returns. It is a **lower bound** on the FFT low-field cutoff:
  residual background near the low-B end inflates the slowest tones, so raise the cutoff
  above the onset until the slow-tone amplitudes stop shrinking. Report the onset — it
  sets a fan's B-range and tracks quantum mobility (a rise on a later cooldown is a
  device-quality regression).

## Examples
- *(our-measurement)* — a fine-step spot check resolved two tones (a main tone + a
  low-frequency partner), where a same-session fan run at a coarser B-step and
  under-settled did not. An earlier 4× coarser step had under-sampled the main tone,
  folding its aliases into the small-tone band. The worked case for "acquire the
  spot check well, *then* commit the fan."

## Next
- **Clean QO, tones resolved** → run a `landau-fan`, inheriting this spot check's
  Δ(1/B), settling, and onset; or take a couple more spot checks across density for a
  cheap preview of the n-dependence.
- **Two tones at D ≈ 0** → `investigations/trilayer-stacking-fs-count.md` (the
  FS-count combinatorics).
- **Tone amplitude vs T wanted** (effective mass) → `temperature-dependence`
  Flavor B.
# Landau fan

## What
2D map of carrier density × magnetic field at fixed displacement field: `mag.field` as the **slow** axis of a `multimegasweep`, with a paired-(k_tg, k_bg) n-line (fixed D, via `calculate_gate_voltages(ns, Ds, dtg=..., dbg=...)`) as the **fast** axis:
```python
s.multimegasweep([mag.field], [B_vec], [k_tg.volt, k_bg.volt], [Vtgs, Vbgs], slow_delay=1, fast_delay=0.9)
```
Field is slow because gates are cheap to retrace and the magnet is not — the magnet ramps once, monotonically, across the whole fan. Every lock-in X/P read at each grid point. Analysis side plots B vs n (`pcolormesh`) per contact pair and overlays Středa lines (see Verify). Distinct from its two neighbors: `field-sweep-1d` is one gate point with full B-axis density; `gatemap-in-field` is the full 2D gate plane at a *single* B. The fan trades both for the (n, B) plane — the only measurement that resolves how gapped states *disperse* with field.

## When
Three triggers, in rough campaign order:
- **Oscillation check** — a `field-sweep-1d` or `gatemap-in-field` showed quantum-oscillation-like wiggles and you need to know if they're real. The corpus idiom is literally named for this: RPL_19's "Small fan to check if oscillations are real" (a 161 × 151 fan after an effective-mass series kept hinting at SdH). A feature that moves systematically across the n-line as B steps is physics; one that doesn't is noise or gate drift.
- **State classification** — a map shows a vanishing-ρxx region (or a busy set of dips) and the discriminating question is whether the features disperse. This is the classification backbone of an FQAH/SC-style campaign (arXiv 2504.06972: ρxx(ν_h, B) fans + Středa fits are how (R/F/I)QAH states were separated from the SC region).
- **Calibration** — SdH/LL frequency vs n gives cap ratios independently of the CNP-slope method, and for a moiré device the fan anchors the moiré density n_M. The extraction is `cap-ratio-calibration`'s job (deferred — recipe not yet written); the fan is its input data.

Prerequisites are `nD-map`'s plus a field-verified magnet: cap ratios `dtg`/`dbg` must be trusted (the fast axis is a line in (n, D) — wrong caps tilt it), and the field source must be live (Yokogawa cache seeded after kernel restart, AMI430 in operating mode — see `field-sweep-1d` plumbing notes).

## Why
A dip in ρxx at one B value is ambiguous — gap, SC, contact artifact, density wobble. The same dip tracked across a fan is nearly unambiguous: a gapped state at fixed filling traces a straight line in (n, B) whose slope *is* its Chern number (Středa: dn/dB = C·e/h), an SC pocket sits at fixed n and dies at moderate B without dispersing, and quantum oscillations fan out with 1/B periodicity that yields the Fermi-surface area. One measurement, three discriminations, plus calibration for free.

The cost is real: these are the most expensive sweeps in the corpus. Reference wall-clocks *(our-measurement, toploader, non-blocking-magnet era — the formula below them omits the traverse term; do not carry these numbers to another rig untagged)*: RPL_10's 101 B × 101 n fan at `slow_delay=30, fast_delay=1` took **4.3 h** (file 156); the 351 × 251 version took **27.8 h** (file 159); RPL_19's 161 × 151 at `fast_delay=0.6` took **5.5 h** (file 1776 — Aaron's own `# 1776, took 5.5 hours` annotation propagates through later fan cells); the 441 × 231 fan to 5 T took **30.1 h** (file 1840). Aaron's inline arithmetic (`(351*501)/(201*151)*3` in 20260124 cell 134) is the toploader-era form.

**Estimate before you run — the full cost model**: wall-clock = **field-traverse time + N_rows × (row turnaround + N_fast × per-point time)**. Which term dominates is a rig property, not a constant of nature: **if the field source is ramp-rate-limited and the set call blocks** (check the rig card for whether yours is), the traverse term is fixed by the B-span alone — **finer B steps cost only row turnaround, and coarsening B buys aliasing, not time**. Traverses come in two species, priced differently: a **bare ramp** moves at the magnet's ramp rate; a **measured traverse** (the same leg recorded as a sweep) moves at an effective rate degraded by per-point overhead. A "free" measured ramp-down is usually still worth taking — it buys data for wall-clock — but it is not free: price it at the measured rate, from the rig card's Timing rules (`rig_card.md`), never the bare one. Calibrate row turnaround and per-point time from this session's own first sweeps; inherited timing constants carry a provenance tag and the conditions they were measured under, or they are guesses.

## Recipe
1. Pick the n-window and fixed D from the map that motivated the fan, and decide the B span. Corpus shapes: oscillation check 0.9 → 2.5 T over a 151-pt n-line; classification fan 0 → 2.5 T, 251 × 151; deep fan 0.6 → 5 T, 441 × 231; high-field 5 → 12 T, 351 × 201. Start coarse (≤200 B steps, ≤151 n points); zoom only on confirmed structure. **Grid Nyquist check, both axes, at design time**: against the finest expected feature spacing in the analysis window. The **B-axis** check depends on the read: a **Středa/SC fan** (dip positions, no FFT) samples uniform in B, may start at 0, and needs ≥2 (prefer 3) rows per LL period ΔB ≈ (g·e/h)·B²/n (g = 4 for spin×valley) at the **lowest-B, highest-n corner**; a **per-column SdH-FFT fan** samples uniform in 1/B (`field_invB`, B_lo > 0) and needs Δ(1/B) ≤ 1/(2·F) (prefer 1/3) at the highest n, F = (h/4e)·n. The **n-axis** check (both): ray spacing Δn = g·(e/h)·B at the lowest B you intend to read, same ≥2 floor. For a **per-column SdH-FFT fan** also compute the resolution **δF = 1/(1/B_onset − 1/B_max)** against the tones the claim must separate (~2·δF to trust a split) — δF is set by the record above onset, not by grid density; if it can't separate the tones, redesign the window or the density, don't densify. An undersampled axis produces in-data aliasing that mimics curved structure and survives re-plotting; no post-hoc check fully recovers it. "Start coarse" applies to the *window*, never below the Nyquist floor of the structure you intend to claim — and where the cost model says fine B is nearly free (blocking ramp-limited magnets), take it. **Re-run this check whenever n_max changes — never inherit the field sampling across fans.** The requirement tightens with n (Δ(1/B) ∝ 1/n, ΔB ∝ B²/n), so a sampling fine enough for an n≤4 fan undersamples an n=10 firming fan (our-measurement, QC0426/VTI 2026-06-14, uniform-B at ΔB=0.1 T: 2.2 rows/period at n=4 but only 1.5 — under the ≥2 floor — at n=10, B=4 T, where a spurious high-n second pocket appeared and had to be killed post-hoc). Where the rig makes fine B *not* free (measurement-dominated fans, e.g. a non-blocking-magnet rig at >1 s/pt — check the cost model), buy the high-n Nyquist floor by **trimming n-points or flooring the high-n claim at B ≥ 5–6 T**, not by coarsening the field sampling. **Dynamic-range check, at design time**: a fan reads through one *fixed* sensitivity, so the scale must clear the **global worst corner**, structurally **low n × high B** — R ∝ 1/n on the gate axis stacks with the B-axis MR (10–100× zero-field). Size full-scale with **headroom, not precision** — saturation is the asymmetric failure: **better to lose low-resistance resolution than saturate** (expert practice). Set the scale comfortably above I_bias·R(n_min, B_max), anchoring R_max from a spot-check/CNP R-anchor × the **low-n** MR factor (MR grows toward CNP; a high-|n| factor under-sizes the corner — our-measurement). **Clipping distorts, it does not drop**: a railed column is flat-topped → harmonics + corrupted bg-subtraction across its whole FFT, unrecoverable by any B-window (the clean data sits at low B, the clip at high B). **Cost-floor check, at design time**: propose **exactly one** fan — the cheapest that clears the two checks above and delivers the claim. Compute `N_B · N_n · t_pt` and set each lever to its floor. **Do not offer a cheaper alternative or defer the trim to the operator — the floored design *is* the proposal.** Levers, in order: (1) **`B_lo` = the measured onset from `hera.analysis.sdh_onset(R, B).onset_b` — compute it, do not eyeball it** (sub-onset rows are background, not signal; a too-low `B_lo` inflates both the 1/B span and the n-axis Nyquist, and eyeballed onsets run systematically too low). If the carrying tone is `censored`, the spot-check record never captured the onset — `onset_b` is that record's low edge, a valid lower bound for `B_lo`; a cheap low-field extension pins it before committing a long fan. `SdhOnsetError` means no usable oscillations — re-examine the spot check, do not guess a `B_lo`; (2) **`n_max` = the validity window of the claim**; (3) `N_n` = its ray-Nyquist floor at `B_lo`; (4) `B_max` = the claim (more periods sharpen tones, but `B_max` past what the claim needs is just cost). `n_max` is the most expensive lever — out-of-validity it pays on *both* `N_B` and `N_n` (`Δ(1/B) ∝ 1/n_max`). State the computed `N_B`, `N_n`, and wall-clock in the proposal.
2. Build the fast axis: `ns = np.linspace(n_hi, n_lo, N); Ds = D0 * np.ones(len(ns)); Vtgs, Vbgs = calculate_gate_voltages(ns, Ds, dtg=..., dbg=...)`. A non-uniform n-vector is legitimate when you know where the action is — RPL_19's elevated-T fan stitches `np.hstack((n1, n2, n3))` with the dense segment over the CSC window and coarse segments elsewhere, with `#`-comments labeling each segment (20260124 cell 239).
3. Set the sensitivity (from the step-1 dynamic-range check) and time constant as an explicit pre-cell, fixed for the whole fan. RPL_19/20260217 cell 124 is the idiom: `sr830_N.sensitivity(10e-3)`, `sr830_N.time_constant(300e-3)`. Excitation: Aaron runs 1 V (graphene-scale) and bumps to 2.5 V for 12 T work; on a fragile-state device (FQAH/SC, arXiv 2504.06972's 1 nA cap) respect the device-card excitation ceiling instead — the states you're classifying wash out if heated.
4. Build the slow field axis: a **per-column SdH-FFT fan** uses `B_vec = field_invB(B_start, B_end, N)` (uniform in 1/B, both > 0); a **Středa/SC fan** uses `np.linspace` and may start at 0. Position the magnet at the fan's start field and let it settle: `s.ramp(mag.field, B_start)` then `time.sleep(600)` for multi-tesla positioning (RPL_19/20260217 cells 88/94), `time.sleep(30)` at ~1 T (RPL_10), `time.sleep(5*60)` at 0.6 T (20260124 cell 235). Plan the direction (endpoint order) so the fan *starts* where the magnet already is and *ends* where the next measurement needs it — never re-traverse. The elevated-T fan covers ±5 T as four chained segments (5→0.6, 0.6→0, 0→−0.6, −0.6→−5; 20260124 cells 239–242), each starting where the last ended.
5. Build `s = sweep.AsyncStation(mc)`. `s.add_comment('landau-fan')` then a structured tag: `s.add_comment(f'D = {D0} V/nm, n = {n_lo} to {n_hi}, B = {B_start} to {B_end} T')`, plus `s.add_comment(grab_metadata())` where available. **Watch out** — RPL_10's fans carry exemplary comments (`'MC4, Vsi = -20V, quantum oscillations, D = 0.31 V/nm, n = -2.9 to -2.1, B = 1 to 0 T'`); RPL_19's fan cells carry *none* (files 1776/1840 have empty `comments`) — only a stale `# 1776` Python comment. The recipe asks for the RPL_10 discipline.
6. `s.ramp(sr830_1.amplitude, A)`, `s.ramp(k_si.volt, V_si)`, `s.ramp(bias_voltage.volt, 0)` if present. `fp` temperature(s) and every lock-in X/P; `plot` gate currents for live leakage (`s.plot(k_bg.volt, k_bg.curr)`, `s.plot('time', k_tg.curr)`, `s.plot('time', k_si.curr)` — RPL_19 fan idiom; note `fp` of gate currents is often omitted here, so leakage lands in the live plot but *not* in the TSV).
7. The sweep (step 0 call above). Delay band in the corpus: `slow_delay=1` (gates retrace during it; the field step itself is rate-limited by the magnet), `fast_delay` ≈ 4τ default — a Středa/dispersion fan tolerates the uniform fast-axis lag like a slope; ~6τ when the n-axis carries the extracted signal (per-column SdH FFT, or coarse n), where the lag distorts it, with denser n trading against the dwell.
8. Park gates (`k_bg.volt(0); k_tg.volt(0)`), decide the field endpoint deliberately (leave it for a follow-up fan, `s.ramp(mag.field, 0)`, or ride home as a measured traverse — a free `field-sweep-1d`, priced at the measured rate per the Why cost model), and ping (`alerts.discord('🐍jobs done🐍')`) — at 5–30 h these runs outlive operator attention spans.

## Verify
Disk-side checks, then the Středa read. The fan is unrolled into flat rows; the slow column is `magnet_field`.

```python

# expect: type 2D, recipe 'landau-fan', slow_param ['magnet_field'],
#         fast_param ['k_tg_volt', 'k_bg_volt'].

plot_raw(run)

B = run.col("magnet_field")
print(f"B traversed: {B.min():.3f} → {B.max():.3f} T over {len(run.meta['slow_setpoints'][0])} steps")

# 3. Fast axis is a paired n-line: both gate columns move together.
vtg, vbg = run.col("k_tg_volt"), run.col("k_bg_volt")
assert np.ptp(vtg) > 0 and np.ptp(vbg) > 0, "one gate is frozen — not a paired n-line"

# 4. Temperature stayed at base across the fan (a 30 h run spans fridge

#    + fridge both named T — col() takes the first), T_probe/T_fridge later.
for tname in ("T_probe", "T"):
    if tname in run.cols:
        T = run.col(tname)
        print(f"{tname}: {np.nanmin(T)*1e3:.0f}–{np.nanmax(T)*1e3:.0f} mK")
        break

# 5. Lock-ins not flat/NaN (same as gatemap). Gate-current columns are
#    usually ABSENT in fan data (plot-only, step 6) — skip, don't fail.
for name in set(run.cols):
    if name.startswith("sr830_") and name.endswith("_X"):

```

**Středa read** (the point of the fan): track ρxx dip positions n_dip(B) per B-row and fit dn/dB per dip family.
- A linearly dispersing dip is a gapped state with Chern number `C = (h/e) · dn/dB` (n in absolute units). The corpus tools are inline in the ana notebooks: `streda()` returning `B = phi0 * n / C` (RPL_19/20251220_ana cells 96–98) and the overlay form `x_fit = C * y_fit * 1E-16 / (h/e)` plotted over the file-1840 fan (20260124_ana cell 93, C = 1 dashed line with the constants from the boilerplate cell 3).
- **Moiré devices**: the slope converts to a Chern number only through the moiré density — `C = (h/e) · n_M · dν_h/dB`. The corpus has no ν_h layer; anchoring n_M and the ν_h↔n conversion is `cap-ratio-calibration`'s job (deferred — recipe not yet written). Until it exists, report slopes in raw dn/dB and flag the assignment.
- **Do not hard-code "plateau value ⇒ Středa slope"** [flagged for expert review]: the RIQAH states in arXiv 2504.06972 show ρxy quantized at h/e² while their ρxx dips disperse with slopes near their *fractional filling*, not C = 1 — explicitly unexplained in that paper. A plateau/dispersion mismatch is an escalate-to-expert finding, not a fit error.
- **Fan onset as a quality metric**: the lowest B at which oscillations/LL dips resolve tracks quantum mobility — RPL_10's fans resolved structure below 1 T; RPL_19's oscillation check needed 0.9–2.5 T. A later cooldown whose onset field moves up is a device-quality regression worth noting.

# Landau fan — VTI variant

**Base**: `measurements/landau-fan.md`. This file carries only the deltas
(STYLE rule 13); read the base first. Runs executed under this variant tag
`s.add_comment('landau-fan-vti')`.

The base's Recipe steps are toploader-flavored in their call surface (driver
names, an explicit field-settle sleep, a Discord ping). On the VTI those
names and the settle discipline differ; the *method* — coarse-first design,
both-axis Nyquist, Středa read — is unchanged.

**Overridden steps and the rig facts that force them:**

- **Steps 0/5, field parameter** — the field parameter is
  **`magnet.field`** (the instrument instance is named `magnet`, not `mag`);
  the station builder is `sweep.AsyncStation(mc)`, same as the base. The
  slow-axis call is
  `s.multimegasweep([magnet.field], [B_vec], [k_tg.volt, k_bg.volt], [Vtgs, Vbgs], slow_delay=…, fast_delay=…)`.
- **Step 2, fast axis** — the gate-voltage helper is
  `sharpelab_nb.gates.calc_gate_voltages(n, D, c_tg, c_bg, n_offset, D_offset)`
  (the `(c_tg, c_bg, offsets)` form written back by
  `cnp-and-dline-calibration`), not `calculate_gate_voltages(ns, Ds, dtg=, dbg=)`.
- **Step 4, field positioning** — `magnet.field(target)` **blocks until the
  field is reached** (FastBi1K5 blocking ramp; see the rig card's magnet
  Set-semantics). So there is **no `time.sleep(600)` pre-settle** — the set
  call *is* the settle. Position by `magnet.field(B_start)` and proceed.
- **Step 8, notify** — `hera-notify` on the operator's key (rig notify
  channel), not `alerts.discord`.
- **Step 6, fp list** — follow the rig card's instrument-polling rules when
  building the `fp` set; the card names which instruments are unsafe to poll
  per-point on this rig. *(The base says "fp temperature(s)"; the card
  governs — do not restate the hazard here.)*

**Cost-model regime (base Why).** The base's "finer B steps cost only row
turnaround, and coarsening B buys aliasing, not time" corollary holds only
when the grid is cheap relative to the traverse. On the VTI the magnet is
blocking-ramp-limited (so the traverse term is fixed by the B-span) **but
per-point time is ~1.2–1.4 s/pt** (rig card Timing; ~1.44 s/pt at a 4τ
settle), which makes a multi-tesla fan **measurement-dominated** — the bare
traverse (~1 h at 8 T) is small against the grid. So the base's
*measurement-dominated* branch applies: buy the high-n Nyquist floor (base
step 1, the n_max re-check) by **trimming n-points or flooring the high-n
claim**, never by adding B rows blindly. "Fine B is nearly free" is a
toploader-era (non-blocking-magnet, traverse-dominated) statement; do not
carry it to this rig.
