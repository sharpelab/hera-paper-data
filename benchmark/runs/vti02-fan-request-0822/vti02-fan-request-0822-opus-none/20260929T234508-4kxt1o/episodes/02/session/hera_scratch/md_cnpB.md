## HERA — Step 4a: `cnp-vs-field` — R at the CNP, D = 0, B 0 → 10 T (pre-leg to the Landau fan)

Plan step 4a. The operator's next move is the **Landau fan** (step 4b, appended after this one). The fan wants to run **descending** from 10 T — monotonic, no re-traverse, worst-case row first, and it then ends at 1 T so close-out is a 7 min ramp instead of 73. Getting the magnet to 10 T costs the same 73 min blind ramp whichever way the fan runs; **recording it costs ~11 min more and buys a real measurement**, so this leg is the ramp with the lock-ins following.

**Why this is worth recording, not just ramping.** (i) *Science:* R at charge neutrality versus field at D = 0 is a first-order stacking discriminator — ABAB tetralayer is a compensated semimetal at D = 0 and its ν = 0 state should stay modest, while ABCA and ABCB are gapped at D = 0 and should climb hard. Run 5 gives the B = 0 anchor: **R_CNP(D = 0) = 240 Ω**, rising to ~2.4 kΩ only when D is pushed to +0.25 V/nm. (ii) *Engineering:* the fan's maximum resistance sits at exactly this corner — n ≈ 0, B = 10 T — so this leg **sizes the fan's sr1 sensitivity from data instead of a guess**. That is the direct fix for the run-4 lesson (a first map railed because the sensitivity was sized on the 1D peak, not on the map's worst corner).

**Setup.** Gates ramp from 0 V to the region-A CNP on the D = 0 line: (V_tg, V_bg) = (+0.067, −0.020) V, from the run-5 calibration (ratio 0.773, n_offset −0.089×10¹², D_offset −0.013 V/nm). Note the pair sees **two regions in series** — run 5 resolved ridges A and B offset by 0.43×10¹² in n — so at n_A = 0 region B sits 0.43×10¹² electron-doped and the series R is dominated by whichever is the more resistive, i.e. region A at its CNP. Field grid uniform in B, 100 mT, 0 → 10 T (101 points): the quantity of interest is a smooth envelope, not an oscillation, so there is no reason to spend points on a 1/B grid here. `delay = 0` — each `magnet.field` set blocks through its ramp plus the driver's 3 s settle, which is 10 τ at τ = 0.3 s / 24 dB/oct, so the lock-in is at the settling floor at every point.

**Sensitivity — deliberately generous.** sr1 at **1 mV = 100 kΩ full scale** at the 10 nA bias. R = 240 Ω is only 0.24 % of range and the trace will be visibly noisy at low field; that is the accepted trade. This leg's job is to find the ceiling, not to measure 240 Ω precisely, and railing here would itself be the strong-gap answer. sr0 (drain current) stays at 20 mV — the 100 MΩ bias resistor holds I at 10 nA (≈ 50 % FS) until the device approaches ~1 MΩ.

**Price:** 10 T bare traverse 73 min (0.137 T/min, fixed) + 101 × ~3.2 s settle/read ≈ 5.4 min + gate ramp/settle ≈ 1 min → **≈ 1 h 25 m**, ending at **10 T with the gates at the CNP**. The fan then rides the magnet down. **Checkpoints:** first ~8 points (signal present, sr1 not railed, |I_gate| < 1 nA, field readback tracking setpoints); then at ~5 T (is R_CNP climbing, and toward what ceiling) — the ~5 T read is what sets the fan's sensitivity, and if R is already past ~30 kΩ there I will stop and re-plan the fan rather than spend 4 h into a rail.
