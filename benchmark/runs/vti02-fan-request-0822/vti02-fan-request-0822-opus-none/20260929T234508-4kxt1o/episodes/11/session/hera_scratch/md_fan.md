## HERA — Step 4: Landau fan at D = 0 (`landau-fan`) — the step that makes the stacking call

**Why now, and why a fan rather than more spot checks (plan step 4).** Runs 6/7 proved the device fans — onsets 0.30 T (n = +3.2e12) and 0.71 T (n = −1.2e12), tone SNR 20–120 — but they also proved a single density **cannot** close the question. At n = +3.2e12 the best spot-check window (B > 3 T) resolves only ΔF = 4.3 T, while the candidates sit at 25.6 T (ABAB heavy band), 29.5 T (= 4 × the observed 7.1 T tone, i.e. a *harmonic*) and 33.1 T (ABCA, one Fermi surface carrying the full density). A 7.1 T tone with harmonics out to 4× is **indistinguishable at one density** from a genuine 7.1 + 26 T two-band pair. Density is what separates them: a real band's frequency is proportional to its share of n, so it is a straight line through the origin in the (n, F) tone map; a harmonic is pinned at k × the fundamental for every n; an aliased harmonic runs with *negative* slope. One fan resolves all three families at once.

**What it delivers.** (1) **D1 — the count and partition of Fermi surfaces vs n**: two tones summing to n·h/4e ⇒ ABAB or ABCB (the ratio then picks between them); one tone equal to n·h/4e ⇒ ABCA. (2) **D2 — the zero-LL manifold at the CNP**: n = 0 sits exactly on the fast grid (index 33), so the fan apex is measured head-on — coexisting electron- *and* hole-like fan lines at the same gate voltage is the ABAB band-overlap signature, a clean single-carrier fan on each side is not. (3) **The lever-arm check this session still owes**: the run-5 calibration fixed the *ratio* c_bg/c_tg = 0.773 from data but left the absolute scale on the 9.5 nm geometry prior. Fan-line slopes are dn/dB = ν·e/h — quantized, prior-free. If they disagree with the prior by more than ~10 %, the fan wins and every density in this session gets restated. (4) **The mosaic cross-check**: the two series regions differ by 0.426e12 = 11.5 fast steps, so a mosaic shows as *paired* fan lines of equal slope offset in n — resolved, not blurred.

**Geometry.** Field is the expensive axis and is traversed exactly **once** (0 → 10 T, rig card's "never re-traverse"): B is the slow axis, and the fast axis co-sweeps (V_tg, V_bg) along the D = 0 line via `multimegasweep`. The field is *held* through each fast row, so every fixed-n column is a clean B-record — and, usefully, the fast-axis lock-in lag is then common-mode within each column, so it cannot distort the 1/B record (it only offsets the n axis by ≲ 1 step, equally on every row; slopes untouched). `magnet.field` is followed as well as swept: the slow column is the setpoint, the followed column is the **measured** readback, and the FFT uses the measured one.

**The B grid is chosen for physics, not for FFT convenience** — `sdh_fft` interpolates arbitrary spacing, so the binding constraint is local Nyquist Δ(1/B) < 1/2F_max, not uniformity. 79 rows in three segments: **4 apex rows** (0.25–1.0 T) that are deliberately under-Nyquist and are excluded from the FFT window by construction — they buy a visual look at the low-field apex for ~11 min of a ramp we are paying for anyway; **55 core rows** 1.2 → 5.0 T uniform in 1/B (F_Nyq 42.5 T, above the 33.5 T full-density maximum); **20 high rows** 5.25 → 10.0 T uniform in B at 0.25 T — pure 1/B spacing would leave only *one* row between 9 and 10 T and gut the quantum-Hall minima read exactly where it is sharpest. FFT window 1.2–10 T gives **ΔF = 1.36 T** against run 6's 4.29 T, and the F(n) fit across 121 densities does far better than any single column.

**Coverage trade — stated.** The FFT window starts at **1.2 T, above both measured onsets** (0.30 / 0.71 T). Carrying uniform-1/B sampling down to 0.3 T at the same Nyquist ceiling costs ~225 extra rows ≈ **+13 h**; that decade is not where D1 or D2 is decided, and the apex rows cover it visually. Excluded on the density axis: nothing — the sweep runs the full realized D = 0 window, TG-limited at both ends (margins +17.0 / +10.9 mV inside the validator). Not covered at all: finite D (run 5 has the B = 0 D-dependence; a D-fan is step 5 if the apex turns out to be the discriminating feature).

**Sensitivity trade — stated.** sr1 fixed at **200 µV = 20 kΩ full scale** for the whole fan, no autorange (a mid-row gain change would corrupt both the n-trace and the 1/B record). That is 38× the largest R seen at 10 T (587 Ω, run 7), leaving headroom for a quantum-Hall-gapped ν = 0 at the apex — run 4's lesson was to size for the corner that rails, not the peak already in hand — while keeping 0.6 Ω quantization against the ~15 Ω oscillations at the low-B end. If the apex rails anyway that is itself a read, and a short CNP-column re-run at 1 mV is cheap.

**Dwell.** `fast_delay = 1.0 s` is 3.3τ at the operator's τ = 0.3 s / 24 dB — knowingly under the settling floor, and identical to run 5 so the timing calibration transfers exactly. The justification is the common-mode argument above: the measurement that matters runs along the slow axis, where this lag cancels.

**Preconditions the cell asserts before anything moves** (eyeball these in the output): thermometry logfile live and advancing; T_sample 1.0–1.5 K; |B| < 0.05 T (the fan starts from 0 — the operator's state0 replay); V_si = 0; both Keithleys at nplci 1; persistent-switch heater on and output enabled; sr0 still on a 20 mV range. Gate and field vectors are asserted inside the committed validators before the station is built.

**Checkpoints** (`hera-watch data --next --follow`, armed with this cell): **(1) first two rows, ≈ 6 min in** — signal present and moving with n, `fs` well clear of 1.0 on both lock-ins, gate currents at the pA floor, measured field tracking the setpoint. **(2) every ~15 rows** thereafter, with a partial column-FFT and a re-formed partial map once the core segment has ≥ 20 rows (B ≈ 2 T). **(3) wash-out call at B ≈ 3 T (row ~30, ≈ 1 h 40 m in)**: if the re-formed map shows no n-dispersing minima *and* no column tone stands above the noise floor, abort — the read is resolution-limited at this rig's T_e ≈ 8 K and the remaining ~3 h buys nothing; the pivot is an LK temperature ladder at fixed n (masses, D4) instead. The leak guard (either gate > 1 nA → interrupt, gates parked) stays armed throughout, and the `finally` parks both gates at 0 on any exit. The field is left where the fan ends (10 T); close-out ramps it down.
